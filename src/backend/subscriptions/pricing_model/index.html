Price needed is what the plan must charge to hit the target margin at full grant consumption — the worst case, where a subscriber burns everything they are entitled to. If it exceeds the actual price, the plan is underwater for power users even while the average subscriber is profitable. Free's revenue is $0 by definition, so its margin is the acquisition cost you're choosing to pay.

built What the code does now

  • One pool, per-member caps. Members hold no wallet; they spend from the org pool bounded by monthly_cap (falling back to the org default, then uncapped).
  • The cap moves inside the debit. debit_credits takes the pool lock and the member row together, so two concurrent actions can't both slip under the ceiling. An over-cap debit is recorded and flagged, never rejected — charge-on-success means the work already happened.
  • Monthly dispersal expires. The sweep rolls the pool each period: this month's volume lands in the expiring bucket, purchased packs sit in reserve and survive.
  • Request → approve raises a cap. No credits move, so approving is idempotent by nature and costs the pool nothing until someone spends.
  • Offboarding reclaims nothing — there was never a balance to take back, just a ceiling.

still open What the contract wrapper needs

The credit mechanics are built; the commercial terms are not. There is no org subscription object, so the 12-month term, the monthly payment, the cancellation grace period and the "remaining months still owed" rule live in the contract you sign, not in the app — an admin buys credits or an operator sets the dispersal.

Also unbuilt: time-gated permissions (only invites expire, after 7 days), and role-gated payment drafting (payouts carry draft/paid but nothing restricts who confirms).

Keeping these honest

This map is a copy — the authoritative table is subscriptions/ai_pricing.py. open.py diffs the two and refuses to open this page when they disagree, so a stale dashboard can't quietly hand you wrong advice.

Real per-call spend is recorded in ai_usage_log via the TrackedOpenAI proxy. Once there's a month of it, replace the modeled token counts below with measured medians.

Where the modeled counts come from

  • OneClick cold: extraction + basis + split-verify on gpt-5.2, plus a mini payable-column detect.
  • OneClick warm: extraction served from the shared parse cache — the two advisory passes are the whole bill.
  • Zoe: routing + answer + citations on mini, escalating to gpt-5 for multi-contract questions.
  • Registry parse: one extraction call, capped at CONTRACT_PARSE_MAX_CHARS (250k ≈ 96 pages).
Prices & grants: live from credit_prices / tier_entitlements / credit_packs. Token counts: modeled.
